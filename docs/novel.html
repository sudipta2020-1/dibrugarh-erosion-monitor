Independent accuracy check labelled data

Test 1. Water mapping against expert labels. The Dynamic World expert-consensus test set (Brown et al., 2022) contains Sentinel-2 tiles of 5.1 km × 5.1 km in which several experts labelled every 10 m pixel by hand. For each tile with both water and land, the same Sentinel-2 scene was mapped by the three methods and compared with the labels. One tile lies on the Brahmaputra at Majuli. The 95 % confidence intervals come from a bootstrap over tiles.

Sub-pixel test. The 10 m labels were averaged to 20 m and 30 m blocks, which gives the true share of water in each block. SWiFT-Bank estimated this share from the block reflectance; a hard water/land map can only say 0 or 1.

Test 2. Bank erosion rates against a global Landsat record. REAL (Langhorst and Pavelsky) gives the bank erosion rate of each river reach from Landsat water maps. The land lost in each reach in Dibrugarh and Majuli was turned into a retreat rate and ranked against REAL. The periods differ, so this tests whether each method finds the same eroding reaches as an independent record, not its exact accuracy.

References: Brown, C. F. et al. (2022). Dynamic World, near real-time global 10 m land use land cover mapping. Scientific Data 9, 251 (test tiles: Zenodo 4766451). Langhorst, T. and Pavelsky, T. Riverbank Erosion and Accretion from Landsat (REAL), Zenodo 7045021.

How SWiFT-Bank works proposed method

SWiFT-Bank stands for statistically tested sub-pixel water-fraction time series for bank erosion. The standard methods give each pixel a water or land label and count whole pixels that changed. SWiFT-Bank instead estimates how much of each pixel is water on every clear date, and then tests whether that amount changed between the two windows.

  1. Water fraction on each date. Each 10 m pixel is a mixture of water and land. On each date, the water end-member w and land end-member l are the mean spectra (green, NIR and sharpened SWIR) of pure pixels within 155 m (MNDWI above 0.3 for water, below −0.2 for land), or within 755 m where pure pixels are few. The water fraction of pixel spectrum x is its projection on the line from l to w:
    f = (x − l) · (w − l) / ‖w − l‖², limited to 0 ≤ f ≤ 1
    Local, per-date end-members follow the sediment load of the river and the sand and crops on the bank.
  2. A sample per pixel. For each window the mean f̄ and variance s² of the water fraction over the n clear dates are kept.
  3. Tested change. The change Δf = f̄now − f̄2020 is tested with Welch's t-test:
    t = Δf / √(s²2020/n2020 + s²now/nnow), with s² ≥ 0.05²
    The false discovery rate over all corridor pixels is held at 5 % (Benjamini and Hochberg, 1995). A pixel counts as land loss when its change is significant and Δf ≥ 0.1, which is 10 m² of land in a 100 m² pixel.
  4. Lost land below the pixel size. The land lost in a patch of k pixels is
    A = Σ Δfi × 100 m²
    Each patch gets a p-value from the pixel tests by Stouffer's method, deflated because sharpened pixels are correlated in 2 × 2 groups: Z = Σ zi / (2√k). Patches with A ≥ 0.02 ha and p < 0.01 are reported.
  5. Detection limit map. For each pixel, the smallest land loss that the test would detect (5 % level, 80 % power) follows from its own noise:
    MDC = (z0.975 + z0.80) × SE × 100 m²
  6. Null test. The current window is split into odd and even dates and the full method is run on the two halves. The 10 m frequency method gets the same test. The area found here is false change, which estimates how often each method raises a false alarm.
  7. Sub-pixel bank lines are the 0.5 contour of the mean water fraction. Bank retreat is the distance between the 2020 and current lines.

What is new. Sub-pixel water fractions (Niroumand-Jadidi and Vitti, 2017), sharpened SWIR (Du et al., 2016) and sub-pixel waterlines (Bishop-Taylor et al., 2019) are already published. To our knowledge, they have not been combined into a monitoring method that (1) estimates water fraction on every date with local, per-date end-members, (2) tests the change of each pixel against its own variability with false-discovery control, (3) reports lost land below the pixel size with a p-value for every patch, (4) maps the detection limit along the bank, and (5) checks its false-alarm rate with a split-window null test on every run. A full literature review is still needed before this claim is published.

Limits. Two end-members cannot fully describe mixed banks with sand, grass and shadow. Wet sand and very turbid water can shift the fraction. The odd/even null test measures false alarms but not missed losses. It is also a lower bound: when real change is present, the false-discovery rule still allows up to 5 % of the significant pixels to be false, so a zero in the null test does not mean zero false alarms in the real map. Field checks at the reported small losses are needed to measure accuracy.
