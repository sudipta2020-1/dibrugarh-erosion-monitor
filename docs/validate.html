How to label (read once before starting)

Each point is one 20 m pixel, marked by the yellow square. Decide what really happened at that pixel between the two periods shown. Use the two Sentinel-2 chips (same months, baseline year and current year) as the main evidence and the high-resolution map for context. The map class is hidden on purpose, so that the labels stay independent of the map.

  • Bank erosion of stable land: land in the baseline image is water in the current image, and the land was a settled, farmed or forested bank, not a sandbar.
  • Char or sandbar lost: land in the baseline is water now, and that land was a sandbar or char inside the river channel.
  • Accretion: water in the baseline image is land (sand, grass or crops) in the current image.
  • Stable land and Stable water: no change between the two images. Crop or vegetation changes count as stable land.
  • Unsure: clouds, shadows or a mixed pixel make the answer unclear. Unsure points are left out of the estimates.

Your work is saved in this browser as you go. When finished, click Download labels (CSV) and upload the file to the repository as data/validation_labels.csv. The next monitoring run for the same window will then compute accuracy and corrected areas. Google Earth Pro (desktop) has dated historical imagery, which helps with difficult points.

No validation sample has been published yet. It is created by the first monitoring run after this feature was added.
